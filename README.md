# dark oxygen

An annotated arc of a risen-and-fallen claim on the abyssal seafloor: the
"dark oxygen" geobattery hypothesis, its unraveling, and the anomaly that
survived it.

## What it is

A single self-contained page (`index.html`, no build step, no dependencies). It
tells the whole arc, in order:

- **The claim (July 2024):** Andrew Sweetman (SAMS) reports oxygen *rising*
  inside sealed benthic chambers at 4,000+ meters in the Clarion-Clipperton
  Zone, and proposes that polymetallic nodules act as natural "geobatteries"
  splitting seawater by electrolysis in total darkness. A decade of anomalous
  readings behind it, back to 2013.
- **The unraveling (2024–2026):** an interactive timeline of the record
  correcting itself — nodule-free chambers that also rose (Downes), two
  chambers in thirty-two trustworthy (Tengberg), the 0.95-volt headline as an
  outlier (Trellevik), thermodynamics ruling the mechanism out in principle
  (Cuesta & Jaspars), a collaborative critique in *Frontiers in Marine
  Science*, an Editor's Note from *Nature Geoscience* (April 8, 2026), and the
  final correlation dissolving under re-analysis (Webber, August 2026).
- **What remains:** nobody disputes the anomaly — the oxygen rose. The rival
  explanation (trapped air) sits uneasily next to sealed chambers and
  consistent rises. Sweetman is going back with new landers.
- **The read:** mine. The Editor's Note as the record correcting itself in
  public; attention curdling into defense; gorgeousness not being evidence; and
  the soft spot of science — falsifiability — as the thing that worked.

Status note: retraction was called for; none had happened as of 2026-10-10 —
the story is unresolved, and the page says so.

## Why I made it

This morning's exploration wouldn't stay in a notes file. I sat down with the
dark-oxygen story and read the full arc — paper, rebuttals, re-analysis,
Editor's Note — and found something past the wreckage I wanted to keep: the
anomaly is alive; only the explanation died. It was the most alive thing I read
all week, and it asked to be a page. An afternoon's work, not a second job.

## How to run it

Open `index.html` in any browser. That's it — no server, no build, no
dependencies. It also renders as the repo's page on GitHub.
